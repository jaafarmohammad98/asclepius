    5. Hypotheses

    For each strong or suggestive finding, the LLM set in Settings proposes how it could come about, from passages of your paper library. Every quote is checked word for word against the paper it cites; one that is not found is removed. These are ideas to test, not conclusions.

    Systems bottleneck map

    The terms of your paper library (genes, hormones, receptors, symptoms) and where PSSD, PFS and PAS converge on them. A line between two terms means they were co-mentioned in the same passages of that many papers, not that one acts on the other; a score says where to look, not a cause. Click a line to read the passages it was counted from.

    Drug repurposing leaderboard

    Approved drugs that act on the top bottleneck targets of the map above, ranked by TOPSIS on five criteria. Every one is a candidate for study, not a treatment: a rank says which drugs might be worth studying, never that a drug helps, and each row lists the risks known of it. A value marked assumed was unknown, and the worst seen stands in for it. The score is TOPSIS closeness (0 to 1) among these drugs alone, under the weights below: it orders them, it measures no benefit. Targets are scored with the default bottleneck weights, not the map's sliders, and the drugs are those fetched for the top targets when the drug data was fetched. The drugs that cause PSSD, PFS and PAS are never ranked.

      Lab experiment builder

      A two-level design over the drugs and targets you tick in the leaderboard and the ranked bottlenecks above, each present or absent in a run. 2 to 4 factors take the full factorial, nothing confounded; 5 take the 16-run resolution V half fraction, whose main effects and two-factor interactions are all separable; 6 to 8 take a 16-run resolution IV fraction, whose two-factor interactions are confounded with each other; more than 8 are refused. The app builds the design, and its seed gives the same run order again. Every combination is a candidate for study, untested, and needs a safety review before any wet-lab or animal use.

        Settings: the LLM that writes hypotheses

        Hypotheses are written by an LLM you choose: Claude with your own Anthropic API key, or a model server on this computer (Ollama, llama-server, LM Studio), so nothing leaves it. Only group-level findings and library passages are sent, never a patient's record. The key is kept in , readable by you alone, and is never shown again.
